# Heterogeneous learner targeted novelty audit

The closest located streams are adjacent rather than equivalent:

- Agrawal, Daskalakis, Mirrokni, and Sivan, *Robust Repeated Auctions under Heterogeneous Buyer Behavior* (2018), studies seller mechanisms robust to different single-buyer behavioral models: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.00494>.
- Agrawal, Balkanski, Mirrokni, and Sivan, *Dynamic First Price Auctions Robust to Heterogeneous Buyers* (2019), studies dynamic revenue mechanisms for heterogeneous behavioral buyer populations: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03286>.
- Feng et al., *Convergence Analysis of No-Regret Bidding Algorithms in Repeated Auctions* (2020), proves convergence results for broad mean-based learner classes: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06136>.
- Aggarwal et al., *Randomized Truthful Auctions with Learning Agents* (2024), studies revenue guarantees and learning-rate effects with no-regret bidders: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09517>.
- Assos, Dagan, and Rajaraman, *Computational Intractability of Strategizing against Online Learners* (2025), includes repeated strategic environments and MWU but asks an optimizer-versus-learner complexity question: <https://proceedings.mlr.press/v291/assos25a.html>.

The general mathematical proposition that heterogeneous algorithms can yield different dynamics is known. The possibly distinct application-level contribution is the ordered-role empirical comparison of FPA versus SPA under Hedge/FTPL/regret-matching mixtures, showing persistent aggregate conflict together with large role effects and revenue reversals at high low-bidder valuations. This audit does not establish priority exhaustively.

Verdict: **POSSIBLY DISTINCT APPLICATION; GENERAL PHENOMENON KNOWN**.
