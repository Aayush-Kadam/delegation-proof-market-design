# Journal strategy

## Primary target: Games and Economic Behavior

Fit: the paper combines auction design, adaptive play, welfare/revenue objectives, and finite-horizon theory. Before submission, emphasize the economic mechanism and move execution engineering to the supplement. Likely objections are the two-bidder setting, finite grids, and the absence of a successful new mechanism.

## Backups

1. **ACM Transactions on Economics and Computation** - strongest fit for the computational/theoretical combination and reproducibility package. Strengthen formal definitions and complexity/reproducibility discussion.
2. **Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization** - suitable if framed around algorithmic delegation and heterogeneous behavioral dynamics. Reduce technical certificate detail in the main text.
3. **Experimental Economics** - possible only with careful positioning as computational experiments rather than human-subject experiments; likely a less natural fit.
4. **AAMAS conference / extended journal version** - suitable for multi-agent learning and role heterogeneity, with a more compact theorem presentation.

No acceptance probability is asserted. The main referee risk is whether a reduced-model theorem plus finite-grid evidence is sufficiently general; the manuscript should foreground scope rather than overclaim.
