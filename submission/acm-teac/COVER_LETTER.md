Dear Editors of ACM Transactions on Economics and Computation,

Please consider “Delegation-Proof Market Design: Revenue–Allocation Tradeoffs under Adaptive Bidding.” The paper asks how standard first- and second-price auction rankings change when bidders delegate repeated decisions to adaptive algorithms.

Prospectively frozen computation shows a recurring objective conflict: first price tends to protect seller revenue, while second price tends to protect allocation and welfare. Exact finite-horizon Hedge results establish the mechanism on explicit parameter regions and richer action sets. Homogeneous Hedge, FTPL, and regret-matching studies preserve the broad effect, while ordered heterogeneous learners expose economically material role effects and revenue reversals. Two natural repair families then fail unchanged prospective gates. A scoped certificate explains the constant-family failure in one explicit environment by proving opposed revenue and allocation derivatives and incompatible safety requirements.

The paper is suited to TEAC because its contribution combines economic mechanism comparison, online-learning dynamics, exact finite-horizon analysis, and auditable computational evidence. It does not claim a universal impossibility or a successful new mechanism. Protocols, code, compact evidence, negative results, and reproduction instructions accompany the submission.

Author identity, affiliation, conflicts, and prior-publication declarations must be verified before submission.
